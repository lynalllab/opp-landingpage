Help us open up mental health data

Could your experience help unlock better mental health treatments?

The problem

Mental health data in the UK is fragmented across many separate resources and secure environments. That makes it hard to connect what we know about genes, cells and molecules with what happens to real people, and it slows down the search for better treatments.

Our solution

The Open Psychiatry Project is building an open, publicly accessible resource that links these datasets safely, so researchers, health professionals and the public can explore the biology of mental health in one place. We want to build it with the people it is meant to serve.

We're looking for people with lived experience of mental illness, and people who care for them, to help shape what we build and how we build it.

We are looking at recruiting 12–16 people with lived experience across our workshop series, including a core group of 3–4 who will stay involved throughout.

There are three tasks to choose from, running one after another between 2027 and 2029. You can join one or all of them.

The tasks

Three projects, one after another across the life of the award, from 2027 to 2029. Each one needs people with lived experience at the table.

  1. 2027 Project 1

    Conversations with science

    Helping AI to unlock the biology of mental health for public understanding.

    What you will do

    You will help us decide what people actually want to ask about the biology of mental health, test early AI prototypes, and define what a “good, trustworthy, and understandable answer” looks like.

  2. 2027–2028 Project 2

    Clues in the records

    Deciding what matters most in hospital & GP data.

    What you will do

    You will review types of real-world health data (from prescription histories to hospital admissions) and help prioritize which specific measures are meaningful enough to invest time in unlocking.

  3. 2029 Project 3

    The Co-Pilot Study

    Leading a team-based mental health analysis, with questions driven by lived experience.

    What you will do

    You will join a collaborative team to pitch research ideas, design a custom study protocol, and test run the secure analysis on the resource.

The workshops will be very hands-on. Each task takes the form of a half-day workshop with 2–3 follow-up meetings, and most sessions will be held online. Never at any time will you be expected to share your own life history — you engage in a way that is comfortable for you.

FAQs

We want to design a digital tool that helps the public, researchers, and patients easily explore mental health data. Instead of experts building something in isolation, we want to build it with you so that it actually answers the questions you care about, in plain language that makes sense.

We have divided the main tasks into 3 different projects:

1. Conversations with science: helping AI to unlock the biology of mental health for public understanding (2027)

Aim

We want to help AI-powered conversational search tools (like ChatGPT, Claude, open source Large Language Models, etc) be able provide accurate answers specifically for mental health science questions, to help people instantly explore the biology of psychiatry without needing to understand complex data systems. But an AI is only as smart as the questions we teach it to answer.

Why it matters

It ensures the AI answers real, human questions (e.g., “How does stress alter inflammation in depression?”) rather than just clinical jargon.

What you will do

You will help us decide what people actually want to ask about the biology of mental health, test early AI prototypes, and define what a “good, trustworthy, and understandable answer” looks like.

2. Clues in the records: deciding what matters most in hospital & GP data (2027–2028)

Aim

Hospital and GP health records are packed with rich, hidden clues about mental health, but the data is messy and disorganized. Instead of trying to clean everything, we want to focus our engineering efforts on cleaning the measurements that matter most to real people and real clinical outcomes.

Why it matters

It ensures the resource tracks symptoms and life events that deeply impact day-to-day quality of life (like sleep disruption or emergency admissions), not just standard blood counts.

What you will do

You will review types of real-world health data (from prescription histories to hospital admissions) and help prioritize which specific measures are meaningful enough to invest time in unlocking.

3. The co-pilot study: directing a patient-led mental health analysis (2029)

Aim

Traditionally, professional scientists choose the research questions. In this project, we are flipping the script. A group led by people with lived experience will completely design and run custom data analyses across multiple secure UK databases using the Open Psychiatry federated data platform.

Why it matters

It represents true equity, allowing public participants to transition from passive advisors to the lead investigators who lead the science.

What you will do

You will join a collaborative team to pitch research ideas, design a custom study protocol, and test run the secure analysis on the resource.

The tasks we would like help with run over discrete time periods. We are recruiting to a network and will call upon people to respond at different times during the project. We estimate that each task would take the form of a half day workshop with 2-3 follow-up meetings.

We keep a project-wide impact register that logs the suggestions and ideas that came from people with lived experience and what happened as a result: whether that’s a change we made, a change still in progress, or something we weren’t able to take forward and why. We’ll share this back with the group regularly, so there’s always a visible line from what you said to what we did.

Your involvement can be voluntary or paid — the choice is yours. For paid involvement, we follow the NIHR public contributor reward policy and offer £27.50/hour, based on the time and preparation each task requires, paid by bank transfer. Most sessions will be held online, though we hope to include at least one in-person workshop, and we will cover any out-of-pocket expenses for attending.
