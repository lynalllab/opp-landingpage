What is the Open Psychiatry Project?

The Open Psychiatry Project is a £2.3M initiative building the UK’s first open, publicly accessible resource for exploring how genes, cells, and molecules influence mental health conditions.

Led by Dr Mary-Ellen Lynall at the University of Cambridge in partnership with Dr Ellen McDonagh at EMBL-EBI, the project extends the globally used Open Targets resource — a major hub for drug and target discovery — to integrate a wide range of mental-health–relevant molecular datasets.

The project also involves establishing links between existing UK mental health patient cohorts, to enable a safe, federated data analysis framework to unify these valuable resources and glean new insights into the biology of mental health using real world data. This will ultimately feed into enhancing the Open Targets resource to provide evidence of potential biomarkers, drug targets, and treatments for mental health conditions.

Our interactive website will become the hub for mental health - presenting links to these resources and clear, accessible information designed for everyone: researchers advancing the science, health professionals seeking new insights, industry partners identifying opportunities, and people with direct lived experience of mental illness

Our goal is to speed up the discovery of better, more personalised treatments for mental health conditions.

Mental health biomarker data is currently fragmented across many resources and secure environments, making it difficult to assess how genes and molecules affect mental health. This project changes that.

How it works

Open molecular data

The resource brings together diverse datasets on how genes, cells, and molecules impact mental health conditions. Researchers will be able to explore biomarkers, drug targets, and potential treatments through a single, open interface.

Privacy-preserving data federation

Using cutting-edge federated technology (in partnership with Bitfount), researchers can analyse data held across multiple secure UK environments without ever moving or centralising sensitive health or genetic information. Privacy is preserved by design.

Co-designed with lived experience

People with lived experience of mental illness are central to the project — co-developing the website's design and content, and providing feedback on the other resources. A dedicated accessible interface will ensure the project outputs are genuinely useful for non-specialist users, not just scientists.

Whether you approach mental health from the bench, the clinic, industry, or your own lived experience — this resource hub will be built for you.

Health Professionals

Discover emerging evidence on genetic and molecular factors that may inform clinical decision-making and patient care.

Industry Partners

Identify validated drug targets and biomarkers to support R&D pipelines in mental health therapeutics and diagnostics.

People with Lived Experience

Access plain-language summaries of the science and help shape how the resource evolves to meet your needs.
